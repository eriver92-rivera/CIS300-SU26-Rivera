Bioactive Enclosures

A bioactive enclosure is designed to create a natural environment that supports dart frogs, tropical plants, and beneficial clean-up crews. A properly built enclosure gives frogs places to explore, hide, and feel secure while also helping maintain moisture and plant growth. Once established, a well-built bioactive enclosure can require minimal intervention because the natural components work together to support the environment.

Building the Foundation

A successful bioactive enclosure begins with a drainage layer beneath the substrate. The drainage layer helps prevent water from remaining stagnant at the bottom of the enclosure. I use black filter sponge mats as my drainage layer because they are lightweight and provide a practical foundation for tropical plants.

Many enclosure builds use materials such as egg crate beneath a layer of soil, followed by mesh and a tropical soil mixture. In my experience, this setup can make an enclosure extremely heavy once glass, plants, soil, water, natural wood, and other decor are added. It can also create problems if the drainage system is not maintained correctly. Stagnant water mixed with soil can lead to bacteria growth, unpleasant odors, unhealthy conditions for frogs, and poor plant growth.

I prefer using a sponge foam mat with leaf litter on top. This approach is lighter, easier to maintain, and suitable for jungle vine plants, moss, and other tropical plants. It also helps create a natural environment without adding unnecessary weight to the terrarium.

Leaf Litter and Clean-Up Crew

Leaf litter is an important part of a dart frog enclosure because it provides cover, helps retain moisture, and creates a natural space for frogs to search for food. Springtails and isopods are also added as the clean-up crew. They help break down waste and organic material inside the enclosure.

Tropical Plants and Hiding Places

Tropical plants such as pothos, vines, and bromeliads can be used to create cover and climbing areas. Plants should be washed and sanitized before being added to an enclosure so they do not bring in pesticides or fertilizers. Half coconut husks, logs, and other natural hiding places can help retain moisture while giving frogs places to feel secure.

Basic Environment

Most dart frogs do well when temperatures remain between 69 and 72 degrees. Humidity should remain above 90 percent. No special lighting is required for the frogs, but proper lighting can help tropical plants grow inside the enclosure.
