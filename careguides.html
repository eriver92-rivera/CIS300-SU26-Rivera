Care Guides

Poison dart frogs can thrive when their enclosure, feeding schedule, humidity, and supplements are properly maintained. This page gives a basic overview of the care practices I use for healthy frogs and stable bioactive enclosures.

Daily Care

Daily care begins with observing the frogs and checking the enclosure. Healthy dart frogs should be active, alert, and responsive to feeding. It is important to monitor temperature, humidity, plant health, and the overall condition of the enclosure.

Feeding

Poison dart frogs primarily eat fruit flies in captivity. Adult dart frogs are usually fed every other day, while juveniles and froglets should be fed every day because they are still growing. Feeding amounts can depend on the size, age, and activity level of the frogs.

Supplements

Supplements are an important part of dart frog care. I coat fruit flies with calcium with Vitamin D3 at feeding time. I also use Vitamin A once a week. Proper supplementation helps support growth, bone health, and long-term wellness.

Temperature and Humidity

Most dart frogs do well when temperatures stay between 69 and 72 degrees. Humidity should remain above 90 percent. A bioactive enclosure with leaf litter, tropical plants, and proper drainage can help maintain stable humidity levels.

Beginner Tips

  • Identify the genus before choosing an enclosure size.
  • Use clean, pesticide-free tropical plants.
  • Provide leaf litter and hiding spaces so frogs feel secure.
  • Watch for aggression, especially between female Dendrobates.
  • Keep fruit fly cultures available before bringing frogs home.
