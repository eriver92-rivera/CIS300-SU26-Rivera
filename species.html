Species Guide

Before choosing a poison dart frog, it is important to identify the genus first. The genus can help determine enclosure size, behavior, confidence level, and whether the frogs are better kept in pairs or small groups.

Dendrobates

Dendrobates are the most popular genus in the dart frog hobby, with over 180 different species. Popular favorites include leucomelas, tinctorius, and auratus. In my experience, they are medium in difficulty and usually range from medium to large in size. They are often confident frogs that may approach the glass and do well when kept in pairs.

Phyllobates

Phyllobates have seven known species and are widely considered some of the largest dart frogs. They are generally easier to care for compared to some other dart frog genera. They are usually larger frogs and can be very bold and confident. In my experience, they work well in small groups of three to six frogs.

One well-known species is Phyllobates terribilis. In the wild, its toxin can be extremely dangerous if ingested. Captive-bred dart frogs do not have the same toxin levels because their diet is different from their natural diet.

Oophaga

Oophaga contain twelve species and are known for their name, which literally means "egg eaters." Oophaga parents feed their tadpoles unfertilized eggs in individual pools of water to help keep them alive. This specialized breeding behavior is one reason why Oophaga are better suited for more experienced keepers.

Oophaga are smaller dart frogs that can be shy and skittish. They need careful attention to enclosure setup, humidity, and cover. They can work well in small groups of three to five when their environment is properly maintained.

Beginner Tip

Female Dendrobates can be aggressive toward each other, so keepers should always monitor for wrestling behavior. This is one reason Dendrobates are often best kept in pairs instead of larger groups.
